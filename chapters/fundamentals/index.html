Fundamentals

The foundational concepts behind everything else in this handbook — how voltage, current, and resistance relate; how digital signals are represented as voltages; and how outputs, inputs, and ground references actually behave in real hardware. Skim this section if you're experienced, but read it carefully if anything later doesn't quite click — it almost always traces back to one of these ideas.

Topics in This Chapter

  1. Voltage, Current & Power

    What the three basic electrical quantities actually represent, how they relate, and how to reason about power dissipation.

  2. Conventional Current

    Why current direction is defined the way it is, and how to keep sign conventions straight when analyzing circuits.

  3. Ohm's Law

    The single most-used equation in electronics — intuition, worked examples, and where it doesn't apply.

  4. Grounding

    What "ground" really means, why it's not a single perfect node, and how poor grounding causes noise and offset errors.

  5. Return Current Paths

    Current always flows in a loop — understanding where the return path actually goes is key to clean signals and low EMI.

  6. Pull-ups & Pull-downs

    Giving undriven digital lines a known, safe default state, and choosing resistor values for speed vs. power.

  7. Open-Drain & Open-Collector

    Outputs that can only pull low — why they exist, and how they enable shared buses and mixed-voltage interfacing.

  8. Push-Pull Outputs

    The default output stage on most digital pins, its drive strength limits, and why two of them should never fight over a line.

  9. Floating Inputs

    Why an unconnected digital input isn't "zero" — and how floating signals cause noise, phantom interrupts, and excess current draw.

  10. Digital Logic Levels

    Voltage thresholds, noise margins, and what really happens when a 3.3 V and a 5 V device need to talk to each other.
